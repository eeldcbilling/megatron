# Week 12 (1–7 Feb): Capstone

*Draft. Tuned at the month-2 review.*

**Why this week exists:** to turn twelve weeks of notes into one document you trade from, and to decide with evidence what the next three months are for.

**Real money:** swing system only.

The Union Budget is usually presented on 1 February. Confirm the date. If it falls in this week, it is your event study: before the day, write what the options market is pricing (straddle price as an expected move, India VIX). Afterwards, compare with what happened.

## Work for the week

### 1. The playbook (`04-journal/playbook.md`)

One document, no longer than four pages:

- Market conditions in which you trade and those in which you stand aside
- Each setup: definition, trigger, stop, exit, size, a chart example of a win and of a loss
- The daily and weekly routine
- Your risk rules as amended
- Your tilt list and what you do about each item
- What you are still weak at

### 2. The numbers (`03-progress/reviews/three-month-review.md`)

- Real trades: count, win rate, average win and loss in R, expectancy, largest drawdown, rule violations by type
- Paper trades in options: the same, separately
- Prediction log: hit rate by week. Did it improve?
- Hours actually spent per week against the plan
- Scores for all concept answers and the three gates

### 3. The oral exam

A one-hour session with the mentor. Twenty questions drawn from all twelve weeks, answered without notes, plus three chart readings and one option position to analyse on the spot.

### 4. The decision

Answer in writing, using your own data:

1. Which style suited your schedule and temperament: swing, options, systematic, or a blend?
2. Does capital go up, stay, or go down for the next phase?
3. Does real-money F&O start? If yes: which one defined-risk structure, what maximum loss per trade, and what must be true first?
4. Is earned leave worth taking now for a focused block? For what, exactly?
5. What are the three goals for the next 90 days?

## Gate 3

- [ ] Playbook written
- [ ] Three-month review with all statistics
- [ ] Oral exam: 70% or more
- [ ] At least 15 real swing trades journaled across the course, with compliance at 90% or more
- [ ] Decision document written and recorded in `00-context/decisions.md`

## Final concept questions

Answers in `06-answers/week-12.md`. These have no model answers.

1. What did you believe about trading on 13 November that you no longer believe?
2. Where does your money come from when you win a swing trade? Who is on the other side and why are they wrong?
3. What is your edge, stated in one sentence that a sceptic could test?
4. Describe the trade from these twelve weeks you are proudest of. It does not have to be a winner.
5. What would make you stop trading altogether, and how would you know that moment from an ordinary drawdown?
